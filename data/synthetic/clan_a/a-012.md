# Lessons learned from the 2025 winter scheduling peak

## Purpose and scope

This summary reviews how the gas nominations process at Aldermoor Energy performed during the 2025 winter scheduling peak. It covers the gas book traded at the Kestrel Hub and the supply nominations that feed from it. The aim is to record what drove late nominations, what changes were tested, and what the team recommends going forward. The review draws on activity logs from Lumen ETRM, the on-premises trading and risk platform used across the front and middle office.

## Background

During the peak, demand from the retail and industrial supply book rose sharply over several consecutive weeks. Nomination volumes changed frequently in the hours before the daily cut-off, and schedulers were required to reconcile positions quickly. Late nominations were the main operational concern, because each one required a revised submission and added pressure on the balancing team.

## Key findings

**Manual edits drove most late nominations.** The review found that most late nominations in winter 2025 came from manual edits made after the 11:30 batch. Automated nomination runs were generally on time. The late submissions were concentrated in the period after the batch, when schedulers adjusted volumes by hand to reflect updated demand forecasts and counterparty changes.

**A freeze window reduced late submissions.** Freezing manual edits 30 minutes before the cut-off cut late nominations noticeably. Where the freeze was applied, schedulers had a fixed point after which no further changes entered the submission, which gave the team time to validate the final file. The freeze did not remove legitimate urgent changes, which were escalated through the duty desk instead.

**Single-person checks were a weakness on busy days.** On the highest-volume days, a single scheduler often made and submitted adjustments without a second check. This increased the chance of keying errors and of corrections arriving after the cut-off.

## Conclusions

The late nominations were not caused by the batch process itself. They were caused by the volume and timing of manual intervention late in the cycle. A defined freeze and an independent check on high-volume days would address both causes with limited change to the existing platform configuration.

## Recommendations

1. Introduce a freeze on manual edits 30 minutes before the nomination cut-off, with a documented escalation route for urgent changes.
2. Require two-person review of all manual nomination edits on high-volume days, as defined in the operating calendar.
3. Track the time of each manual edit in Lumen ETRM so that late-cycle changes can be reviewed after each peak.
4. Review the effect of these changes at the close of the next winter period and report the results to the scheduling steering group.

## Next steps

The scheduling team will confirm the freeze timings and the high-volume day criteria with the operations lead before the next winter period. Updated procedures will be published to the shared operations library once agreed.
