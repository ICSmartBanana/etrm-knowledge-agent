# Lessons learned from the 2025 netting disputes

## Purpose and scope

This summary reviews the netting disputes Brightwater Refined Products handled during 2025 across its diesel, jet fuel and gasoline trading book. It is intended for the trading operations, credit and legal teams, and it records the conclusions and recommendations that came out of the review. It does not replace the netting agreements themselves or the internal close procedures that apply to them.

## What we found

The review found that most netting disputes in 2025 arose from a single cause: counterparties applied different cut-off dates when calculating the amounts to be netted. One side measured positions as at the close of business on one date, while the other side used a different date, often because the two firms' settlement or booking calendars differed. Where the cut-off dates diverged, the same set of trades produced two different net balances, and each side had a reasonable basis for its own figure.

Disputes of this kind were slow to resolve. Teams had to reconstruct which trades each party had included, reconcile timestamps in the trading and settlement systems, and agree a common position before any payment could be released. Several cases also tied up collateral calculations while the figures remained open.

## What worked

The most effective change was agreeing the netting date explicitly in the confirmation for each affected trade or master arrangement. Once the cut-off date was written into the confirmation, the counterparties had a single reference point, and most disputes that had previously recurred did not reappear. Clear wording at the point of trade proved far cheaper than resolving the question after settlement.

## Recommendations

1. Require every new confirmation and amendment to state the netting cut-off date, and check it at booking before the trade is released.
2. Review existing confirmations with active counterparties and add the netting date where it is missing.
3. Build a standard check in the Corvane CTRM workflow so that a trade without a netting date is flagged for the operator before settlement runs.
4. Hold a short cut-off calendar review with each counterparty at the start of each quarter, so that differences in business calendars are identified early.
5. Log every netting dispute with its root cause, so that future reviews can measure whether cut-off mismatches continue to decline.

## Next steps

Operations will table the confirmation template change at the next trading controls meeting. Credit and legal will confirm the wording with the counterparties most exposed to the issue. The team will report back on progress in the next quarterly review of the netting dispute log.
