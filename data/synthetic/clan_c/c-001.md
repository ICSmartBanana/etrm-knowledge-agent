# Cerrado Agri Commodities trading operations overview

**Effective date:** 2025-10-01
**Document type:** Overview
**Audience:** Internal staff, who may quote this document

## Purpose

This overview describes how Cerrado Agri Commodities conducts its trading operations. It is intended as a reference for staff in trading, logistics, risk, and finance who need a shared understanding of what the company trades, how contracts are denominated, and which systems support the business.

## Commodities traded

Cerrado Agri Commodities is an agricultural and soft commodities trader. The company trades four commodities:

- coffee, sugar, cocoa and soybeans

Supply is sourced primarily in Brazil and West Africa. Sales are made mainly to European roasters and processors. Because sourcing and sales sit in different regions and time zones, the business depends on consistent contract records and clear handoffs between origin teams and sales desks.

## Units of measure

Physical contracts are written in metric tonnes. This applies to all four commodities and is the default unit for pricing, quantities, and delivery terms.

Coffee is also quoted per 60 kg bag. Staff quoting coffee to customers or suppliers should state the unit explicitly, and confirm whether a price refers to metric tonnes or to 60 kg bags before any figure is shared outside the company. Unit confusion is a common source of pricing errors, so quotes should never rely on an unstated unit.

## Trading platform

All trade capture, position tracking, and contract lifecycle management take place in Harvest CTRM, the company's commodity trading platform. Key practices include:

- Every physical and derivative trade is recorded in Harvest CTRM before it is confirmed to a counterparty.
- Contract quantities and prices are entered in the unit specified in the signed contract.
- Amendments to executed trades are made through the platform's amendment workflow so that the audit trail remains complete.
- Staff should use Harvest CTRM as the single source of truth for open positions. Spreadsheets or email summaries are not authoritative.

## Operational responsibilities

Trading desks are responsible for accurate trade capture and for confirming terms with counterparties. Logistics teams track shipments against contracted tonnage and report variances promptly. Finance teams reconcile invoices against the contract unit and quantity recorded in the platform.

If a discrepancy is found between a contract, the platform record, and an invoice, staff should stop further processing of that item and escalate it to their line manager. Do not correct a platform record directly without the approval required by the amendment process.

## Questions

Questions about this overview or about how a commodity is denominated should be directed to your line manager in the first instance.
