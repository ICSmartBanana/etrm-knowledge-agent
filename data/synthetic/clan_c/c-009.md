# Counterparty Sanctions Screening Policy (Version 2)

**Company:** Cerrado Agri Commodities
**Document type:** Policy
**Effective date:** 2025-03-01

## 1. Purpose and Scope

This policy sets out how Cerrado Agri Commodities screens its counterparties against sanctions lists. Cerrado is an agricultural and soft commodities trader covering coffee, sugar, cocoa, and soybeans. We source from Brazil and West Africa and sell to European roasters and processors. The policy applies to every counterparty the company trades with, including suppliers, buyers, agents, logistics providers, and financing partners.

Sanctions screening protects the company from dealing with restricted parties, keeps our trade flows lawful, and supports the reputation our customers and suppliers rely on.

## 2. Screening Requirements

### 2.1 Routine Rescreening

All counterparties are rescreened against applicable sanctions lists every 6 months. This routine cycle applies to all active counterparties, whether or not their trading volumes have changed.

### 2.2 Screening Before New Contracts

Every counterparty must be screened before any new contract is signed or any new trade is confirmed. A clean result from the most recent routine cycle does not replace this pre-contract check. Trade desks must not confirm a new contract with a counterparty whose screening is pending, incomplete, or flagged.

### 2.3 High-Risk Jurisdictions

Counterparties located in, or shipping through, high-risk jurisdictions are rescreened monthly. The Compliance team maintains the current list of high-risk jurisdictions and updates it when sanctions designations change. Monthly rescreening applies in addition to the routine cycle in Section 2.1 and the pre-contract check in Section 2.2.

## 3. Screening Process

1. Counterparty details, including legal name, registration number, beneficial owners, and known trading names, are recorded in Harvest CTRM before screening begins.
2. Compliance runs the screen against the applicable sanctions lists and records the date, the lists checked, and the outcome.
3. Matches and possible matches are escalated to Compliance. No trade may proceed until the match is cleared or the counterparty is rejected.
4. Cleared results are stored against the counterparty record so that the next screening date is visible to traders and Compliance.
5. Where a counterparty's ownership, address, or jurisdiction changes, an ad hoc rescreen is performed before the next trade.

## 4. Roles and Responsibilities

- **Compliance** owns the screening process, maintains the high-risk jurisdiction list, and makes final decisions on flagged matches.
- **Trading desks** must confirm that screening is current before entering any new contract and must not work around a pending or flagged result.
- **Operations** keeps counterparty records in Harvest CTRM accurate and up to date.

## 5. Recordkeeping

Screening records, including dates, lists consulted, outcomes, and escalation decisions, are retained in line with the company's document retention schedule. Records must be complete enough to show an auditor what was checked, when, and by whom.

## 6. Breaches

Trading without current screening is a breach of this policy. Breaches must be reported to Compliance immediately, and the relevant trades must be reviewed before any further activity with that counterparty.
