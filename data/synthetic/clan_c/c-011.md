# Lessons Learned from 2025 Provisional Invoice Adjustments

## Purpose and Scope

This summary reviews how provisional invoices were adjusted to final invoices across Cerrado Agri Commodities' coffee, sugar, cocoa and soybean contracts during 2025. It is intended for commercial, operations and finance teams who prepare or approve provisional invoicing in Harvest CTRM. The conclusions apply to physical cargoes sourced in Brazil and West Africa and sold to European roasters and processors.

## What Drove the Adjustments

Across the year, the largest share of final-invoice adjustments traced back to weight differences recorded at discharge. Provisional invoices are typically issued on loading or shipping weights, and the quantity that arrives at the buyer's warehouse or plant can differ for reasons that include moisture loss, handling losses, scale calibration and bag count variances. Where the weight basis was not clearly defined in the contract, these differences became harder to reconcile and took longer to settle.

Price-related adjustments, such as final pricing against an agreed index after the pricing period closed, were a smaller contributor and were generally predictable once the pricing schedule was in view.

## What Worked

Appointing independent surveyors at both the loading and discharge ends was the single practice most clearly associated with fewer disputes. When both parties relied on a common, neutral weight and quality record, the discussion moved quickly from whether a difference existed to how it should be applied. Teams that agreed the surveyor and the weighing method at contract stage reported the fewest escalations.

## Recommendations

1. **Define the weight basis in every contract.** State whether provisional and final quantities use shipped weight, landed weight or a stated tolerance, and name the weighing method.
2. **Use independent surveyors at both ends by default.** Make this a standard clause rather than a negotiated extra, and record survey reports in Harvest CTRM against the relevant contract.
3. **Flag expected weight variance at invoicing.** Where a shipment type has a history of discharge differences, include a note on the provisional invoice so that the adjustment is anticipated rather than surprising.
4. **Review adjustments monthly.** Operations and finance should review open provisional invoices together, grouping them by cause so that recurring patterns are visible early.

## Conclusion

Weight differences at discharge were the main source of 2025 final-invoice adjustments, and independent surveying at both ends reduced disputes. Adopting these two practices as standard across all commodity lines should reduce the volume and duration of future adjustments.
