# ETRM Implementation Playbook

## Purpose and Scope

This playbook sets out the generic approach Meridian ETRM Consulting uses when implementing energy trading and risk management platforms for clients. It is a best-practice reference for consultants and project staff. It contains no client-specific information, and it may be quoted freely within the company. Project teams should adapt the steps below to each engagement's contractual scope, client governance and platform configuration.

Effective date: 2025-10-01

## Standard Implementation Phases

A typical ETRM implementation runs in five phases: discovery, design, build, test and cutover. Each phase has defined entry and exit criteria, and the project steering group should confirm exit before the next phase starts.

**1. Discovery.** Consultants gather business requirements, map current trade capture, confirmation, settlement and risk reporting processes, and identify data sources and integration points. Outputs include a current-state assessment and a list of gaps against the target platform.

**2. Design.** The team defines the target process model, data model, workflow rules, reporting layouts and interface specifications. Design decisions are logged with their rationale and the stakeholders who approved them.

**3. Build.** Configuration, customisation and interface development take place against the approved design. Changes outside the design go through the formal change control process.

**4. Test.** Unit, system and integration testing run first, followed by user acceptance testing. Defects are triaged by severity and tracked to closure.

**5. Cutover.** Production data is migrated, interfaces are switched over, and the platform goes live under a documented go/no-go decision.

## Requirements Baseline

Freeze the requirements baseline at the end of design. Once frozen, the baseline changes only through formal change control, which records the impact on scope, schedule and cost. Teams that skip this step typically find that build and test effort grows with every late request, so the discipline is worth the effort.

## Dress Rehearsals

Run at least two full dress rehearsals before cutover. Each rehearsal should execute the complete cutover runbook end to end, including data migration, interface switch-over, reconciliation of positions and balances, and rollback. Record timings for every step and fix any issues before the next rehearsal. Treat the final rehearsal as the reference for the go/no-go decision.

## Good Practice Summary

- Keep the requirements baseline under control from the end of design onward.
- Document every design decision and its approver.
- Plan the cutover runbook early, and rehearse it fully rather than in parts.
- Involve business users in testing, not only in requirements gathering.
