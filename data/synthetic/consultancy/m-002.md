# ETRM Upgrade Checklist

**Document type:** Checklist
**Owner:** Meridian ETRM Consulting Knowledge Base
**Effective date:** 2025-10-01
**Audience:** Implementation and support staff. Clients may be given a copy of this checklist.

This checklist is a generic playbook for planning and carrying out an upgrade to an ETRM platform. It contains no client-specific facts. Adapt each step to the environment, the release notes, and the change approval process for the engagement in question.

## Purpose

Upgrades to energy trading and risk management systems touch trade capture, valuation, settlement, and reporting. A disciplined checklist reduces the chance that an upgrade disrupts end-of-day processing or downstream invoicing. Use this checklist from the planning stage through to post-go-live review.

## Before the Upgrade

- **Take a full database backup** before any change is made to the environment. Confirm that the backup completes and that it can be restored in a non-production location.
- **Record the current version** of the platform, including application, database, and any integrated modules or interfaces. Store this record with the change ticket.
- Review the vendor release notes and identify any changes to configuration, data models, or interface formats.
- Confirm that the upgrade has been approved through the client's change control process.
- Notify the operations and support teams of the change window and the expected impact.

## Testing

- **Regression-test end-of-day, invoicing and scheduling jobs** in a copy of production. Compare outputs with the baseline from the current version, including record counts, totals, and job completion times.
- Test trade capture, valuation runs, and position reports against a representative set of live-like trades.
- Test all inbound and outbound interfaces, including confirmations, settlements, and general ledger postings.
- Log every defect found, assign an owner, and resolve or formally accept each one before proceeding.

## Cutover and Rollback

- Schedule the cutover outside of trading hours and end-of-day windows.
- Keep a rollback window of at least **48 hours**. During this period, the backup and the prior version must remain available so that the environment can be restored if a critical defect appears.
- Define rollback triggers in advance, such as failed end-of-day jobs or invoice discrepancies that cannot be corrected quickly.
- Document the rollback steps and rehearse them on the copy environment.

## After Go-Live

- Monitor the first several business cycles closely, including end-of-day, invoicing, and scheduled job runs.
- Hold a post-upgrade review to capture lessons learned and update this checklist where needed.
- Close the change ticket only after the rollback window has passed without a rollback decision.
