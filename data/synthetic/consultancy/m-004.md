# Settlement and Invoicing Best Practices

## Purpose and Scope

This guide sets out generic good practice for settlement and invoicing in energy trading and risk management (ETRM) and commodity trading and risk management (CTRM) environments. It is part of the Meridian ETRM Consulting knowledge base, which holds reusable playbooks and best practices for implementation and support work. It contains no client-specific facts. Effective date: 2025-10-01.

## Contractual Terms Come First

Payment terms, netting rules and dispute windows are set by each client's contracts, not by the platform. Consultants should never assume a default term from a system configuration or from another client's engagement. Before configuring settlement logic, confirm the governing agreement, including:

- The payment due period and how it is counted.
- Whether and how positions or invoices may be netted.
- The window in which a counterparty may raise a dispute, and what evidence it must provide.

Where the contract is silent or ambiguous, escalate to the client's legal or commercial owner rather than inferring a rule.

## Recommended Settlement Workflow

### 1. Reconcile Before Release

Reconcile confirmations against invoices before release. Each invoice line should trace to an executed confirmation for the correct counterparty, product, quantity, price and delivery period. Investigate and clear any break before the invoice leaves the system, because a corrected invoice after release is harder to manage and may restart dispute timelines.

### 2. Separate Duties

Separate the duties of invoice preparation and approval. The person or process that builds an invoice should not be the one who authorises it. A typical control pattern includes:

- Preparation by the settlements or back-office team.
- Independent review and approval by a different role, such as a settlements manager or controller.
- A system-enforced check that the preparer cannot approve their own invoice.

### 3. Record and Monitor

Keep an audit trail of changes, approvals and releases. Track open disputes against the contractual window for each counterparty so that deadlines are visible to the team well in advance.

## Practical Tips for Consultants

- Document each client's terms in the configuration specification, with a reference to the source contract.
- Avoid hard-coding terms that may change at renewal; use parameters maintained by the client.
- Test netting and dispute logic with representative examples before go-live.

Consult the client's contract register and the implementation's reconciliation procedure for client-specific details. This guide is generic and should be adapted, not copied verbatim, into any engagement.
